✦ Common Questions

What people usually ask before they book

Short, practical answers about intake, treatment fit, timing, and the first few steps after contact.

Booking

Do I need to know which therapy I want before booking?

No. Most new patients start with a conversation or intake that helps the clinic decide whether functional medicine, acupuncture, nutrition, herbal therapy, or a combined path makes the most sense.

First Visit

What usually happens during the first appointment?

The practitioner reviews your symptoms, timing, history, current routines, and any previous care. That first session is usually about building context, identifying priorities, and deciding what the first practical phase should be.

Preparation

Should I bring lab work, imaging, or a medication list?

Yes, if you have them. Recent labs, scans, medications, supplements, or even a short timeline of symptoms can make the first visit more useful and reduce repeated questions later.

Treatment Fit

Is this more wellness-focused or more medically structured?

It is deliberately both. The atmosphere stays calm and supportive, but the clinical thinking remains structured around symptoms, history, diagnostics when needed, and measurable follow-up instead of vague recommendations.

Treatment Fit

Can I combine therapies, or do I need to commit to one approach?

You can absolutely combine approaches. Many care plans start with one clear entry point, then expand into nutrition, acupuncture, bodywork, or herbal support once the initial priorities are clearer.

Routing

What if I only know my symptoms and not the right practitioner?

That is normal. The clinic can route you based on your main concern, whether that is fatigue, digestion, stress, hormones, pain, recovery, or child-focused care. You do not need to solve the routing problem alone.

Follow-Up

Can I start with one issue and build a broader plan later?

Yes. Many patients begin with one concrete issue such as sleep, digestion, fatigue, migraines, or cycle support. Once the first phase settles, the clinic can widen the plan into a longer-term recovery or maintenance path.

Timeline

How quickly do people usually notice change?

That depends on the concern, how long it has been present, and whether the first goal is relief, diagnosis, or deeper recovery. Some people notice change quickly, while more complex cases need a steadier, phased plan over time.

Contact

What if I am not ready to book but want one direct answer first?

You can send a short message first. The front desk can usually tell you which practitioner fits best, whether an intake is the right first step, and what information would be useful before booking.

Need direction?

Start with the concern, not the modality

If you are comparing options, use the symptom to narrow the first step instead of trying to choose the full long-term plan immediately.

Fatigue, hormones, or complex cases → Functional Medicine
Pain, migraines, or stress load → Acupuncture
Digestion, food structure, or energy dips → Nutrition
Child routines, immunity, or feeding → Pediatrics
